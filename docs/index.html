Uptake held through the summer peak and rose in autumn

Bookings through the RTHA engine, split by whether a SAF contribution was included: 72% in July, 79% in October.

The pre-selected 10 % does the work: 69% kept or raised it

What travellers did with the default contribution. Opt-out fell from 28% in July to 21% in October.

Where SAF was kept on, travellers covered 11.8% of their emissions on average. Uptake was identical on mobile and desktop (75%), so the default, not the device, drives it. Average contribution where kept on: €77 per booking.

Long leisure routes carry the impact

CO₂e avoided by destination, tonnes, top 8 of 12 routes.

Five Iberian routes — Barcelona, Alicante, Faro, Málaga, Lisbon — account for 61% of all CO₂e avoided.

London City is a top-4 route by volume (392 bookings) but, at 320 km, avoids only 6.3 t: short routes are where a rail option matters.

Proposed for next quarter

Make certificates shareable

Show rail next to London City

The shortest route avoids the least CO₂e; a rail option keeps the sustainable choice honest.

Method. All figures come from a synthetic dataset of 3,753 bookings built for this case study, not from real RTHA or BookBetter data. Emissions 0.12–0.17 kg CO₂e per passenger-km by distance; SAF via Book & Claim, 2.0 kg CO₂e avoided per litre (~80% lifecycle reduction), travellers pay the €2.00/L price gap; 2.5% revenue share is illustrative.
