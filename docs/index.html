What travellers did with the 10 % default

Share of 3,753 bookings. The default is pre-selected; travellers can raise, lower or switch it off.

Opt-out fell from 28% in July to 21% in October. Where SAF was kept on, travellers covered 11.8% of their emissions on average and paid €77 per booking.

What this means for RTHA

  • The default does the work. 69% of travellers kept or raised the pre-selected 10 % contribution; only 25% switched it off, and that share is falling.
  • Sustainability is now measurable per route. 107,733 litres of SAF and 215 t CO₂e, each backed by a certificate — figures RTHA can report publicly.
  • The engine pays for itself. €1.74M booked and €43,610 in revenue share, with 1 in 8 bookings already from a returning traveller.

Proposed next quarter

  1. Test a 15 % default on the five Iberian routes, where uptake is highest — an A/B test on conversion and opt-out.
  2. Make certificates shareable (1,871 issued): a “my flight was flown on SAF” moment for social and for RTHA’s own channels.
  3. Show rail next to London City flights: the shortest route avoids the least CO₂e per booking; a train option keeps the sustainable choice honest.
Method. All figures are computed from a synthetic dataset (3,753 bookings) created for this case study — no real RTHA or BookBetter data. Emissions: 0.12–0.17 kg CO₂e per passenger-km by distance band. SAF: supplied via Book & Claim, 2.0 kg CO₂e avoided per litre (~80 % lifecycle reduction), travellers pay the €2.00/L price gap. Partner revenue share of 2.5 % is illustrative. Source code and data: github.com/slvtveter/bookbetter-rtha-impact-report
