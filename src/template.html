What this means for RTHA

  • The default does the work. {{ kept_or_more | round0 }}% of travellers kept or raised the pre-selected 10 % contribution; only {{ c_off.share_pct | round0 }}% switched it off, and that share is falling.
  • Sustainability is now measurable per route. {{ t.saf_litres | int_ }} litres of SAF and {{ t.co2e_avoided_t | round0 }} t CO₂e, each backed by a certificate — figures RTHA can report publicly.
  • The engine pays for itself. €{{ t.gross_booking_value_eur | eur_m }} booked and €{{ t.partner_revenue_share_eur | int_ }} in revenue share, with 1 in 8 bookings already from a returning traveller.

Proposed next quarter

  1. Test a 15 % default on the five Iberian routes, where uptake is highest — an A/B test on conversion and opt-out.
  2. Make certificates shareable ({{ t.certificates_issued | int_ }} issued): a “my flight was flown on SAF” moment for social and for RTHA’s own channels.
  3. Show rail next to London City flights: the shortest route avoids the least CO₂e per booking; a train option keeps the sustainable choice honest.
